### 0.1 Where Episode 5 sits in the arc
Ep1 "The Hollow Throne" (Fire vs Shadow, Obren falls, Malrik takes the heart-stone) -> Ep2 "The Light Below" (Ice joins
Shadow, Ysolde's army destroyed, Nature joins Fire: 2v2) -> Ep3-4 the Battle of Evenmead (the same morning from both
hills) -> **Ep5 "Gold for Fire": the war goes south.** Evenmead cost both alliances their treasuries and half their
armies. In the far south, beyond Vessa's own kingdom, two harbour cities have hated each other for a thousand years;
since the Master's one public step-in they have fought only by a yearly duel (and by every dirty trick the duel's bets
allowed). Now his chair on the Tollrock is empty, the dragons that lived in his sky have flown home to the Coinwater,
and the old price is back: **whoever pays the most gets the fire.** Malrik and Torvald sail for Adamarch; Vessa and
Ysolde for Aurivane. The episode is an auction that becomes a war: gold, dragons, two champions who would rather keep
their honour than their cities' grudge, and **four apprentices the Master trusted to bring peace, arriving with gold,
like everyone else**. JS's line "they only saw the money" is meant to read both ways: the cities then, the four now.

## 1. The lore reconciliation: the world's policeman who could not stop one war
**JS's ruling (2026-09-27, relayed):** the Master tried to be the world's police; the harder he tried, the less it
worked; he kept experimenting until he trusted the four wizards to bring peace, but they only saw the money.

How it fits the prologue: **the prologue is the Master's own "once upon a time"** (he narrates it as a legend, then
"I was that wizard"). For most of the world it is true: kings brought him their quarrels, his sky was full of dragons
that never breathed in anger. **The Coinwater is the page he left out**: the one war that ran through the whole
thousand years of his peace, and outlasted every way he tried to stop it. Ossavar opens the history with exactly that:
*"They say he kept the peace for a thousand years. Not here."* Nothing in the prologue is contradicted; one old man's
bedtime story simply skips his failure.

The chronicle (years counted back from the night of the cold open):
| When | What happened |
|---|---|

What this adds to the canon: the Master was not a flawless god-king; he was the strongest wizard of all time and still
could not make two greedy cities stop. Every tool he tried (gold, storms, taking the dragons, even his own face and
word) was turned into more war, and that is **why the four exist**. Episode 5's irony is JS's line read both ways: the
cities only saw the money, and so did the two apprentices he trusted with them. Old Vey (the Master's old sword) and the
Oath-orders fit under it: the Laurel Duel's rule, "first blood, and no further", is the oath the Master later asked of
every sword he trusted (flavour for the Hollow Oathsworn / Greenheart Oathkeeper cards; no gameplay change).

## 2. The two city-states
Both are **harbour cities of gold and diamond, for the rich and strong only**: nobody is born a citizen. A citizen must
**pay** (the gate price) **and prove** (a Spartan-style upbringing: children leave home at seven for the training
grounds; whoever fails at twenty loses the city). Everyone else lives outside the wall in the **Outwall**, the poor
harbour quarter whose dockers, fishers and rowers keep both cities running. Their motto, carved over both gates in
different words: *gold opens the gate, blood keeps the house.*

| | **Aurivane**, the Gold City ("the Sun Gate") | **Adamarch**, the Diamond City ("the Diamond Wall") |
|---|---|---|
| Where | South shore of the Coinwater: ochre-and-gold cliffs facing north | North shore: a black basalt headland facing south |
| Wealth | Gold mines in the cliffs (glowing adits); the strait toll when it holds the Tollrock | Diamond pits terraced into the headland; cut-stone trade to every kingdom |
| Colours | **Gold and crimson**, black trim | **White and silver, sapphire sea-blue** |
| Emblem | **The Sunblade**: a golden sun whose lowest ray is a sword | **The Fist and Stone**: a white diamond held in a mailed fist |
| Gate price | **The Scale**: at the Sun Gate a newcomer is weighed against gold and must pay their own weight | **The Stone**: a newcomer must work a year in the pits and cut a diamond with their own hands, then buy a house |
| Training (Spartan) | **The Anvil**: a sun-baked drill yard; spear, shield and sword from dawn to dusk; the children sleep on the sand | **The Whetstone**: cold sea swims at dawn, sword drill in silence, one word a day; the Hall of Silence |
| Architecture | Terraces cut into the cliff, **gold-leaf domes**, colonnades, red awnings; the **Sun Gate**: two 40 m gilded warriors cross their spears over the harbour mouth; the **Victory Stair**, one step per year of war, 1000 carved steps; the Treasury Dome on the summit | White marble and glass on black rock; **faceted towers** with cut-glass crowns; **the Facet**, a diamond the size of a house on the Admiralty spire, throwing rainbows over the strait; a **round war harbour** (a circular basin ringed by ship sheds, the Admiralty tower on an island in its centre) |
| Ships | Galleys with **crimson sails** and bronze sun-rams | Galleys with **silver-blue sails** and crystal-capped prows |
| Soldiers | Crimson cloaks, crested gold helms with cheek plates, round **sun-disc shields**, long spears | Silver scale, sea-blue cloaks, tall **faceted kite shields** with a crystal boss, long swords and halberds |
| Ruler | **The Gold Archon**: the richest citizen who also wins the Anvil trial, chosen every year | **The Diamond Archon**: chosen for life by the Hall of Silence |
| Champion | **Dacian Orrow, the Sunblade**: loud, laughing, generous; a two-handed sword with a sun-gold edge | **Seraphine Vael, the Diamond Wall**: quiet, exact, never wastes a move; long sword + faceted shield that throws rainbows |
| Temperament | Glory: loves the crowd, the laurel, the song | Pride: loves the record, the rule, the silence after the win |

**Why they fight (gold and glory)**: the **Tollrock** in the middle of the strait holds a gold vein and the only
lighthouse; whoever holds it taxes every ship that passes (gold). Each city carves its victories as **laurels** on its
walls and counts them against the other's (glory). Since the Truce the laurels are won by duel instead of by battle, and
wagered on; the count never lets either city forget.

**How the dragons switched sides**: dragons never belonged to a city. They take **hoard-bonds**: a dragon serves the
city that fills its hoard, until the other city fills it higher. When the Master called them north (experiment 3), the
cities simply raised the price; the hoards grew into the cities' biggest debts (Aurivane's gold domes, Adamarch's pits).
Ossavar: 200 years for Aurivane, then Adamarch paid more; then back, then again: **twice each** (the gold ring on his
broken horn is Aurivane's, the diamonds in his scars are Adamarch's). War year 612, the Year of Two Skies: every dragon
at once. Only the Master's public step held them (in his sky, ~130 years); last year they came home, and the trade is
open again. Gameplay keeps this rule (section 7: the Dragon Auction).

### 3.1 The duel, beat by beat (what the choreography must show; Motion Lab hero clips, section 6)
The duel is about **honour and backbone**: both are brilliant, neither cheats, and the ending is respect, not murder.
1. **Entrances** (D03-D04): Dacian drags the Sunblade's tip through the sand from the south gate, crimson cloak, and
   raises it to the Aurivane half of the crowd (a roar in gold and red). Seraphine walks in from the north gate without
   a glance at the crowd; the noon sun hits her faceted shield and **throws a rainbow across the sand**.
2. **The bow** (D05): both turn and bow to the Master's stone chair, **empty for the first time**, a white cloth on it.
3. **First clash** (D06): Dacian opens with a huge overhead cleave; Seraphine angles the shield so it slides off in a
   shower of sparks; sand sprays.
4. **The flurry** (D07): three fast two-handed blows drive her back to the arena wall; spectators lean over the rim.
5. **Shield bash and the strap** (D08): she bashes him off balance; he spins with the fall and **cuts her shield strap**;
   the shield flies and spins flat in the sand (ECU: the diamond boss throws a last rainbow).
6. **Sword against sword** (D09): now even; an orbit of fast exchanges; she flicks the crest plume off his helm; he
   laughs out loud.
7. **The bind** (D10): blades locked at the hilts, faces close: "Yield, Diamond Wall." / "Walls don't yield."
8. **The breath** (D11): they break apart to opposite ends; the crowd goes silent; only wind and gulls. Both charge.
9. **The pass** (D12): they cross at full speed, one flash of steel (top-down); they stop back to back, still.
10. **First blood** (D13): a thin red line opens on Dacian's cheek. The herald: "First blood! Adamarch!" Half the
    crowd roars, half groans.
11. **Honour** (D14): Dacian touches his cheek, grins, and offers his hand: "Well struck." She takes it.
12. **The shadow** (D15): a vast shadow sweeps the sand; every head turns up; silence. **Ossavar lands** on the arena
    rim above the empty chair; the wind of his wings tears the white cloth off it. Dacian and Seraphine, without a
    word, turn **back to back, blades up**: the two champions defend each other before their cities.

## 7. The playable side: map `two_harbours` and Episode 5 mission 1 "The Dragon Auction"
**Map idea**: the Coinwater strait runs west-east across the middle; the Tollrock arena island sits in the centre inside
a ring of water; **Aurivane's quarter** is the south shore, **Adamarch's** the north. Each side's players build in
front of their city's walls (the city itself = a big non-buildable landmark block behind the keeps, with its harbour
as a gold trade post). Crossings: two causeways to the Tollrock, two shoal fords ("Gold Shoal", "Silver Shoal"), two
flank lane bridges. **2v2 = fourfold** (partners share a shore), **1v1 = `two_harbours_duel.map`**, the same shore
geometry with `mirror_z` and one keep per side.

Mechanics (all on existing systems: GoldBank / trade, CapturePoints, EpicBeasts, the Crown carry rule):
| Mechanic | Rule (draft numbers) | Built on |
|---|---|---|
| **The Dragon Auction** (JS's "dragons switching side") | From 8:00, every 6 min Ossavar (or a `gilt_dragon` / `prism_dragon` in skirmish) lands on the Tollrock for 45 s. Each team bids **gold** at its own Bazaar; highest bid wins the dragon for 3 min as an allied beast (it attacks what the team attacks). Next auction the other team can outbid: **the dragon switches sides.** Ties go to the team that holds the Tollrock | EpicBeasts / BeastBrain (team swap), GoldBank, Market UI |
| **The Tollrock** (the old prize) | A capture point with the lighthouse: its holder gets the **strait toll** (+gold per trade caravan crossing any crossing) and wide sight over the water | CapturePoints (blessing = toll), TradeNetwork |
| **The Laurel** | Once per match (at 12:00), a champion duel ring opens in the arena: each team may send one champion or T2 sworn blade; first blood wins **+1 laurel = 300 gold + a 60 s army blessing** | Crown-style event, champions |
| Rich harbours | Each city's harbour is a trade post with double gold income; the Outwall shanties hold 2 extra crystal nodes each (the poor quarter feeds the rich city) | TradePost, nodes |
| City guards | Neutral Gilded Spears / Facetguards garrison each city gate and fight whoever attacks it; a team that has "won" its city (bid >= 1000 gold total) gets them as allies for the rest of the match | NeutralCamp leash, team swap |
